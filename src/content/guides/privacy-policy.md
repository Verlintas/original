A privacy policy is not a magic page that makes data collection legal. It is a description of what you actually do. Start with the data map, then write the page.

## MAP THE DATA

For each item, write down:

- what you collect
- why you collect it
- where it is stored
- who can see it
- how long you keep it
- how someone can delete it

If you do not collect something, say so plainly.

## WHAT TO INCLUDE

- who operates the site or app
- what data is collected and why
- cookies and similar technologies
- analytics, advertising, and embedded third parties
- email and contact forms
- legal bases, where GDPR applies
- retention periods
- sharing and processors
- international transfers, if any
- user rights and how to exercise them
- children, if relevant
- how changes are announced
- contact details

## COMMON MISTAKES

- Copying a policy from a company with a different data model.
- Claiming “we do not collect data” while using analytics or a CDN that logs requests.
- Listing rights that do not apply in your jurisdiction.
- Forgetting email forwarding and support inboxes.
- Never updating the policy after adding a tool.

## A SMALL-SITE DEFAULT

If the site is static, has no accounts, no analytics, and no cookies, say that. Then describe the hosting provider's request logs and the email provider used for contact. That is a short, honest policy.
