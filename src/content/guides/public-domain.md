Public domain means no copyright owner can stop you from using the work. But the rules depend on the country, the date, the author, and the type of work.

## COMMON ROUTES

- copyright has expired
- the work was never eligible for copyright
- the author dedicated it to the public domain
- a government publishes it as public domain (not universal)

## CC0 AND UNLICENSE

- **CC0-1.0** is a public-domain dedication with a fallback license. It is designed for all kinds of works.
- **Unlicense** is a public-domain dedication written for software.
- **0BSD** is a permissive license, not a public-domain dedication. It still relies on copyright law.

## BE CAREFUL

- A work may be public domain in one country and protected in another.
- Translations, annotations, and new editions can have their own copyright.
- “Free to read” is not the same as public domain.
- A photo of a public-domain painting may still have rights in the photograph.

## WHERE TO LOOK

Use official sources and record the provenance. The [public sources directory](/guides/directory/) lists archives and libraries. For software, read the license text; for creative works, check the rights statement on the source.
